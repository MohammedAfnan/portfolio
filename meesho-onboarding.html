Work / Reseller onboarding, hackathon audit

A reseller’s first order shapes whether she stays. I audited that flow against her own goals.

Meesho lets anyone share clothing and accessories with their contacts and earn a margin on each sale. I followed a new seller from her first open to her first order, and found three places where the app works against what she is trying to do.

Problem

The brief was open: pick one flow and explain why. I picked the first order.

A good first experience makes people more likely to keep using a product, recommend it and try something else from the same brand. For a new reseller, the first order is that experience. If it goes badly, she may not try again.

Share products easily

Send a product to the right people on the app they already use.

Keep track of orders

Know which customer wanted what, across chats and channels.

Save time

Reselling sits next to a job, study or home. Every extra step costs her.

Two kinds of first-timer:Type A has never resold anythingType B knows the rough process

Who I audited for

A first business, run alongside work, study or home.

Most Meesho resellers are housewives and young women. Tech comfort varies a lot. Some only use WhatsApp and Facebook, others already pay online without help. I defined the persona before opening the app, so the audit had something to measure against.

The two users I checked every screen for

Type AHas never resold anything. Knows only that the app pays out.
Type BHas a rough idea of reselling and expects the app to follow it.

What already works

Five screens passed cleanly.

An audit is fairer when it says what to keep. These screens already help her and the proposal leaves them alone.

Gender pickerIcons over text, with a clear exit.
Step completeShows exactly where she is.
PaymentCash on delivery next to online options.
AddressFields check as she types.
Order summaryEditable, with a clear status.

Issue one: sharing

Tap share, and the app sends her to WhatsApp twice.

Once for the photos and once for the description, with no cancel. Pressing back by mistake sends her to WhatsApp again to finish a share the app thinks she left. Her first goal is to share easily, and this step makes it harder.

Two notes on what works, two on what doesn’t.

Issue two: sign-up

A sign-up dialog appears mid-flow, and one wrong tap loses it.

If she dismisses it, there is no route to checkout. She is left on the product page without a next step. Saving time was one of her goals, and a dead end works against it.

Issue three: checkout

Checkout comes before there is a customer.

In real selling, she shares a product, waits for someone to show interest and then places the order. The trial order skips the wait and goes straight to checkout. A first-timer may take that as the normal order of things and get confused the next time she logs in. With no link to a customer or a channel, tracking orders gets harder from the very first one.

How reselling works

She shares the productthenA customer shows interestthenShe checks out the order

What the trial order teaches

She shares the productthenNo customer yetthenShe checks out the order

A hint that checkout waits for a customer would have been enough.

The proposal

Map the real process first, then fit the app around it.

Real reselling has six steps: check availability, share, wait for interest, collect details, get paid and place the order. For a first-timer I reduced these to three. Select and share, wait for interest, then place the order with a margin. Her profit reaches her account after delivery.

Current flow and proposed flow

Each critical issue gets a direct change.

This was a hackathon entry, judged against other entries over one weekend. I have no evidence that Meesho adopted any of it.

Current flowProposed flow
Sign-up appears mid-flow and is lost if dismissedSign-up happens first, before the process starts
Onboarding video is compulsory for everyoneVideo is optional, with a stepper for first-timers
Share jumps to WhatsApp twice, with no cancelOne share action she starts, with a cancel
One channel assumed per share, with extra buttonsOne menu, with WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook treated equally
Checkout right after sharing, with no contextCheckout tied to customer interest, with shared products in their own list
No record of which channel a customer came fromCustomers and orders tagged by channel

None of this was measured. It is a proposal, and the next step would have been putting it in front of resellers.

Outcome

A jury pick as runner-up, and an idea that still needs testing.

How I counted

Issues: I checked each screen against usability heuristics for Type A and Type B. Three broke the flow, two were minor (including the compulsory video) and five passed.

Steps: the six real-world steps came from mapping how resellers sell outside the app. The three app steps are the ones a first-timer has to act on.

Jumps: today one share opens WhatsApp twice. In the proposal she starts each share herself.

Result: picked as runner-up by the UX Hack jury in April 2021, in a separate round from my WhatsApp entry. Nothing here was measured with real resellers.

What I’d do differently

Two things I’d change next time.

Test with real resellers

I’d put the proposed flow in front of two or three Type A and Type B users before settling the structure.

Check the sign-up move

I assumed asking for a phone number at the very start doesn’t raise drop-off. I’d want to test that, not assume it.
