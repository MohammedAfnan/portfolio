Is everyone in every centre?

I rebuilt Proctra’s live exam dashboard from a survey of five exam co-ordinators, so attendance reads centre by centre.

Problem

One session can hold 432 students, 42 proctors and 17 test centres. The old dashboard added them all into one set of totals.

Totals, not a roll call

Students and proctors showed as session totals, split male and female. Nothing said which centre was missing people.

Absent was hard to find

Students not reported sat in row three of one card. Proctors not reported sat in row two of another.

Alerts lived in a drawer

Notifications opened in a side panel with no severity. A proctor login looked as loud as a student caught copying.

Closing was a guess

Answers not uploaded was the last row of the last card. Nothing stopped a co-ordinator closing the session early.

One persona, one job:Exam co-ordinator at the control desk during a live onsite session

Qualitative and quantitative research

Before I changed anything, I sent the same survey to five exam co-ordinators. Open questions told me what worried them. Fixed questions told me how many.

All five ran onsite sessions on the old dashboard. I wrote the fixed questions from the old screen itself, one for each thing a co-ordinator does with it. The open questions asked about worry and about the last session that went wrong.

Raised by 5 of 5

Show me each test centre on its own

A session total of 245 students hides a centre with nobody in it. They wanted to read attendance room by room.

Raised by 5 of 5

Keep male and female centres apart

They run them as separate halls with separate staff, and the exam office expects separate reports.

Raised by 5 of 5

Present and absent, for students and proctors

Absent mattered more than present. An absent proctor means a room of students with no one watching.

Raised by 4 of 5

Tell me when something breaks

Failed uploads and dropped connections were found late, often by a phone call from the centre.

Red flags and errors

I listed every way a live session can go wrong, then gave each one a state on screen.

Red flags are people problems, like an absent proctor or a flagged student. Errors are system problems, like data that did not arrive or does not match. The list came from the open answers and from walking through the old screen with the co-ordinators.

How I counted

Centres: the old dashboard had no per-centre attendance. That lived in the test centers tab. The new one has a card for each of the 17 centres, grouped 8 male and 9 female, three shown per group with the rest one tap away.

Scenarios: 6 red flags and 5 errors, listed in the section above. Each has a severity, a place on screen and an action.

Headline numbers: the old screen showed 50 numbers across four cards (students 27, test centres 6, proctors 9, assessments 8). The new one leads with 4 ratios and 8 exception counts, then the centre cards.

Survey: 5 exam co-ordinators, 5 fixed questions and 3 open ones, sent before any design work started.

Hindsight

Four things I’d change next time.

Run the same survey after launch

I asked five people before the redesign and nobody after. Question 2 on the new screen would have given me a real before and after score.

Time a task, not only ask

A survey records what people say they check. Timing “find the centre with no proctor” on both screens would show what they actually do.

Ask more than five

Five is enough to see a pattern and too few to trust a 3 against 2 split. I’d aim for 12 to 15 across small and large centres.

Rehearse the errors with a real session

I designed 11 scenarios on paper. I’d stage a mock exam, pull a network cable and send a proctor home, and watch the co-ordinator react.
